---
title: "No Provides Feedback"
date: 2026-03-19
excerpt: "I didn't expect to end up at an EF selection day."
---

I didn't expect to end up at an EF selection day. I applied on a whim, thinking nothing of it, wrote down about my more *adventurous* past experiences for the craic. 

I joked about these experiences, explained why I'd chartered the course I had, and one-by-one I went through the interview process -- each time surprised to be asked back. 

So as the selection day came up, I wasn't sure what to think. A mixture of imposter syndrome, quiet confidence, excitement and fear enveloped me. 

A long journey to Shoreditch, no coffee in my system, I walked through the doors and immediately got a comment on my physique. I have learned to flip what used to lead to frustration into gratitude. 

I was introduced to the team, scanned the room, and headed straight to the coffee machine (of course).

At the start, it's hard not to make passing judgements on people. Having a model in my head of who each was, I'd seen a *blurb* of their life -- **the most impressive things they'd done** -- at least what looked to be so on paper. 

I didn't move past this yet. Breakfast: too small for my stomach, too big to hold in one hand; I began with a near disastrous drop. 

Small talk led to small introductions in a room where our blurbs were reiterated. I've never been good at *selling* myself. A skill I've worked on, but still lagging. I never think what I've done is quite *good enough*.

Five-minute intros. Much better. More comfortable one-on-one. But, just too short to get into the good stuff. 

I enjoyed trying to avoid the typical conversation route, the low-resolution speed-dating question of, "Tell me about yourself." Instead, the aim was to steer it towards how a person thought, and how they saw the world. 

You can get a surprising amount of information in five minutes if you pay attention. 

After almost two hours of this, I was drained. Straight into sprints.

I grabbed the person I thought aligned with my view of the world the best and got to work. We theorised what a world might look like in five to ten years and worked backwards towards the question, *"What does that world need that it does not yet have?"*

We made progress, and maybe if time permitted, we would've made more. But, in the pressure cooker of a selection day, you don't have time to stretch before you hit your top set. We split up. 

I saw: *I must get more comfortable with no.* Not only a no from someone else, but saying no myself. 

Each yes brings opportunity cost. 

I'm retraining my mind to associate a *yes* with suspicion and a *no* with relief. 

I want to move to a bigger pond. At least a different pond. You can question whether I should, but that's beside the point. In order to do so, there's a whole period of rejection which follows.

No provides feedback. Yes tells me nothing. 

Staying in the same pond is where I get told *yes*: yes, let's work together; yes, that work is good enough; yes, you are already enough. 

The last one -- dangerous. An innocent phrase, but due to its personal nature, my mind can twist it to justify all sorts of inaction. 

Travelling between ponds requires I learn to get more comfortable with *no*. 

He was better suited to the remaining straggler without a sprint partner, so I went solo the rest of this day. 

I looked into edgeAI problems in the agriTech space. Highlight was going round interacting with others not on the clock. 

Like Claude Opus, when people don't think they're being *assessed*, you learn far more about the person beneath the blurb. 

At times I began to wonder, "How am I in this room?"

The night wrapped up with pizza too long for the plate, one too many coffees, and a final *sprint* (more like a jog) into the night. 

Time to go home. 

Day two: break-ups besides mine (finally) -- I quickly scouted a new partner in sprint. 

Problem space: food&beverage manufacturing. Didn't thrill me, but I ran with it. To my surprise, it taught me more than a safe within my 'edge' would have. I realised the unique ways my networks intersected and I leveraged them as best as I knew in the moment. 

Admittedly, sometimes I fabricated connections! I'd call, they'd ask, "What's up?" I would fumble through questions, wasting both our time, instead of admitting circumstances had changed. 

*Constraint is the mother of creativity; we're built to solve hard problems, not complex ones.*

This has been said before.

It's one thing to know, another to feel. 

The central lesson I take into this next period of my life from the two days.

The first day my partner and I flapped our wings a little. We stayed in the abstract, the *not clearly defined* territory. 

The place where you can look productive because there's no quantifiable way to disprove it. **You're making a lot of movement, you're just not going anywhere.** 

This is a habit I can easily fall into. 

I like a lot of things! Shiny-object syndrome runs rampant on my life. I get *good enough* at things to talk about them at parties, but not quite good enough to make a career of them. 

I bounce between path-to-path, pretending the complexity of my life's journey balances the sub-power results from it. 

Simulations ran in my head and not in reality; justifications which lean on an unhealthy dose of *main character syndrome*; talking about a new goal each week and sticking with none of them. 

If *no* is where I gain information, and I repeatedly short-circuit this by switching course at high frequency, then: I will never change.

It's not a bad life. It's a forgettable one.

Heading back to clearly defining territory. Whether I succeed or fail, I guarantee: I will at some point hear the word *no*, and I will collect a learning for the journey beyond. 

The day continued killing hypotheses one after the other.

Each interesting connection could connect me to as many interesting people as I already knew.

Day two ended with multiple sprints, a quick summary of the biggest *could-be 'secrets'* learned, and a trip to the pub. 

I had fun. 

Thank you to all those who were there for making it so. 

I didn't get in. I got told *no*. And surprisingly, that brought a smile to my face. 

I hadn't been told no in a long time. Too long. 

I plan to go deep on three areas: edge and local AI, poetry and blogging, and fitness. 

All areas I am good enough to speak about at parties. 

All areas I plan to get good enough at, so the next time I have to proffer up my *most impressive achievements* list, they will be on it.
