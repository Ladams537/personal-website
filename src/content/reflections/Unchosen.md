I have managed to get sick three times in four weeks. This might be a new low for me.

I was at Chris Williamson's show in London, where someone asked a question about involuntary suffering, and I guess this is a small dose of that.

I've always handled the suffering I choose well. The suffering I don't choose, however, I can so quickly turn to bitterness.

It's funny. In the past, when I've noticed this, I do what most of us would do -- I try to root out the bitterness. I accept it is not good and that I ought to change.

Usually, I succeed. Victory won!

However, in due time I realise all I have done is substitute it. I've gone from a more obvious bitterness to a less obvious, more destructive one, which on the surface the world is more accepting of.

Many choose to succumb to the vices that choose them.

Often those who are great successes in one field are not to be envied in others. That's the bargain obsession demands.

These past four weeks: I've been insignificant yet greater than all others; I've thought myself dumb, then, a moment later, a genius; I've been present for moments then gone for days.

Maybe right now my mind is still unable to think clearly, my words incomprehensible, my prose tangled.

Yet, in this state of sickness, I am reminded -- maybe when I am "better" it will be no different.

Thoughts I think so smart are mere dribbles to another.

How, in health, can I fall for the illusion that my version of reality is all there is? That this is it?

That this is all the five senses can detect. That this is all a human can be.

How the world eats us up, and we forget, in more moments than we remember, that we are but specks of dust in comparison to infinity, and beneath that lies the realisation that one ought to simply be: unrestrained, free.
