---
title: "Empty Pockets"
subtitle: "Week 8"
date: 2026-11-22
---

I was ready to leave the nest. By the nest I mean the sofa I had lived on for a month.

Somebody made room for me for that month. I noticed almost none of it while it was happening.

I think of myself as someone who cares, and I do. It just tends to arrive late, after the door has closed, when there is nobody left to tell.

It is worse when we disagree. Something small goes wrong between us and every kindness they have ever shown me goes missing. I know it is all there. I cannot reach any of it. I stand in the argument with empty pockets.

I only understood why in Hermanos.

The wifi had gone down. I closed the laptop, which I had not done in front of another person in months, and there was a face where the screen had been.

We started swapping favourite memories.

I keep a list of mine. I made it alone, and I thought it was a good one.

Across that table it fell apart in minutes. They would mention a street and I was back on it. A meal. Something I said on a walk home. None of it was on my list and all of it was better.

An hour at that table took us further than the months before it.

So that is where I keep things. Not in words. In people and in places, and it comes back when I am standing in front of them. My body remembers long before my head catches up.

Which explains the arguments. It also explains this week.

I have just moved, and everything my body knew how to do lived in the old rooms. Where the keys go. Which way to turn in the dark. Who I was at that desk. Here I reach for a light switch and find wall.

Transition periods. There is enough confusion in them, and enough loose energy, to start down a new path: better or worse.

For a few weeks nothing here is habit yet.

I don't know what I will make of that. I know the first thing I want to learn in these rooms, before they settle and I stop seeing them: to remember people while they are still in front of me. To find something in my pockets during the argument, and not a week after it.

So, to the owner of the sofa: thank you. I should have said it from the cushions.

And to the small table, and the face across it. To the streets that came back the moment someone named them. To the laughing. I was carrying all of it, and could not find it when it counted.

Next time I am stuck, that is what I would like to come to mind: the people, the rooms, the life already lived. All of it, and in time.
