Some years pass by and are remembered for days. Some days pass by and are remembered for years.

A friend seeking a blueprint for discipline asked how I remained so "dialled in" to the gym during my first year of university.

My routine was a clockwork ritual: 5:00 AM alarms, 6:00 AM lifting sessions, and calorie counting. My free time wasn't spent relaxing; it was spent consuming educational content to further the craft. I did this six times a week. "Off days" were the hardest to endure.

I didn't experience burnout. I didn't tire of the monochromatic existence I was living. I wasn't being disciplined—I was obsessed.

Obsession is rarely a conscious choice; it is the convergence of passion and pain. It turns the "I have to" or even the "I want to" into an absolute "I must." It is an inexorable alignment of curiosity, identity, and purpose.

A common saying is that the most dangerous man is the one with nothing to lose. I would argue his younger brother is just as formidable: the obsessed man. While having something to lose, he is so blinded by his goal that he acts as if the world around him doesn't exist.

If life hands you a good obsession, don't let the world's desire for balance slow you down. Run with it. Life is short, and such clarity is rare.

Furthermore, an acquaintance was recently asked for a reference on me.

Having known me since in that first year, he described me as "one of the most diligent and disciplined people" he knew.

Objectively, he wasn't wrong, yet I squirmed.

It wasn't until I read a thought from Chris Williamson that I realised why: Discipline is not obsession, though they are often identical to the observer.

Discipline is a daily negotiation—a grit-teeth fight against the self. Obsession is a surrender—a total submission to the current.

What once required a voracious, conscious effort now requires none at all.

My current discipline is simply the echo of my past fire. The furnace was built years ago; now, I am simply staying warm by the embers.

What once obsessed me has now simply become me.

There is a paradoxical freedom that comes once the "Must" has been satisfied.

When you are in the teeth of an obsession, you are a closed system. But once that obsession matures into your foundation, you gain a strange new permission: the ability to look up from the path.

This was captured this week when I found myself at a concert for the wrong artist. First-year me would've loathed this. Current me had an awesome time! It reminded me that while obsession builds the foundation, it is the release of that obsession that allows for serendipity.

And now I stand in a liminal space.

I appreciate the warmth of past achievements, but at twenty-two, "staying warm" feels dangerously close to standing still.

I am waiting—perhaps even begging—to be struck across the face by another obsession. I am looking for a problem with enough gravity to pull me into its orbit.

I’ve learned the hard way that you cannot make a person that gravity.

In the past, I tried to make love the "Must" itself. It never works. It is too much weight for one spirit to carry; eventually, the pedestal breaks, and you both fall.

Love is not the obsession; it is the reward for having one.

It is the moment where vision meets its sharpest allies: aptitude, ability, and acuity.

If you are truly fortunate, you don’t find someone to distract you from the mission. You find the one who sees the flicker of your vision and demands that you fan it into a blaze.

Until that next "Must" arrives, I remain a traveler. I haven't found my next summit yet, but sometimes I wake up and think the mountain is already beginning to rise beneath my feet.
