title: "Borrowed Years"
subtitle: "Week 29"
date: 2026-03-15
---

A week of rejection, waiting, and death. 

I started at EF selection day with a mix of exceptional people. 

I was in the right room because I felt I shouldn't be there. 

I had fun holding pressure, channelling flow, and stepping back from action into presence. 

I ended the two days in a state of gratified tiredness.

I waited to hear back on how I'd done. I expected the worst, hoped for the best. 

In the space of uncertainty I let aimlessness beat me up. 

Then rejection came. 

Followed by another rejection.

Followed by the death of my Granny's last living sibling. 

I wanted to explain it away. 

Minimise the pain by saying, "it's part of the plot."

Control the narrative, control how I perceived things. 

Cause this is what I know how to do. 

I know how to move onto the next thing and forget about the last. 

I know how to let memories eat their way through my unconscious mind and into my everyday -- because that's tomorrow's problem. 

But pain, if captured, can be the catalyst for change. 

I know this. 

Yet, I don't sit with it. 

I escape it. 

Initially this tastes like a shallow contentment. Then the aftertaste hits. 

Weights get heavier the longer you hold them.

And I'd been holding mine for weeks. Months. Stacking goals on top of goals, frameworks on top of frameworks — as if complexity was proof I was serious. 

As if the more variables I juggled the closer I was to deserving the life I wanted.

I'd rather be broke than breaking.

So I stripped it back. Not out of wisdom. Out of exhaustion. 

I stopped asking "what should I be?" and started asking "what can I actually test?" 

Simple inputs. Measurable outputs. 

Things I can look at in three months and know — not feel, know — whether they worked.

Because the opposite of pain isn't comfort. It's clarity.

And clarity, it turns out, is boring. 

It doesn't look like a revelation on a hilltop. 

It looks like a kanban board. A decision made on a Tuesday. 

A pebble which becomes a stone which becomes a rock which becomes a mountain.

A life with direction. 

Harry lived longer than anyone expected. 

Borrowed years, my Granny called them. 

And he didn't spend them solving his life. He just lived the one he had.

Drinking, smoking, wearing three-quarter length joggers into his eighties, and flirting with ladies in the nursing home until his dying day. 

I'm twenty-two. 

I have the luxury of killing my character again and again until I find me. 

Not the version of me my younger self thought I should be. 

Not the version of me the world most values. 

Me.
