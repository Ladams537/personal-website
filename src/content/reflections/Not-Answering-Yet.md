With perfect information comes perfect condemnation.

That's what I'd have told you on Thursday.

My thoughts come and go. Thrown at me so quickly I get whiplash, and if I am unlucky enough to catch one, I take that thought for me.

A stranger I'd give the benefit of the doubt. I'd assume a long day, a backstory, a reason. Myself I judge on the raw feed.

I call that perfect information. It isn't. I see every thought and none of the work behind it.

---

A button came off a pair of trousers this week. Someone once mended them for me. A late-night coffee craving that was once someone else's. That was enough. A day of noise, two small moments. 

On Thursday night: an argument. Tired. Every thought arriving dressed as a verdict. I didn't sign any of them. 

Time is often the solution, I told myself. 

I know this in the gym; one rep at a time. You can't rush it. You can't solve the problem faster by working out for eighteen hours every day. The growth happens in the rest.

---

So maybe Thursday's line is wrong.

Condemnation doesn't come from knowing everything. It comes from knowing one moment completely and mistaking it for the whole.

The conscious mind isn't the judge. He is the labourer. He works long days, sleeps little, and is mostly wrong about what will happen next. He does the unglamorous work in the foreground so something in the background can try combinations until one fits.

My job is not to believe him when he hands me garbage at midnight. It's to wait, and then seize the moment when the background surfaces what couldn't be rushed, and might never appear again.

---

And yet, a life thought out is not a life lived. 

Some answers surface on their own. Some questions only get answered by living them, and waiting is a choice too. I don't know yet which kind I'm holding.
