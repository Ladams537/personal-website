Time leaves us little choice but to act. 

Maybe if we all had enough time, we would all find the courage within us to seize the day. 

Is there a right path?

Some days it’s as if somewhere in-between falling asleep and rising again I became the loam soil for a flower to blossom. Other days I wake up degraded, good for nothing but further decay.

Eventually, after only finding holes of a past life, I stop digging.

What replaces passion, conviction, and drive is not what I thought. It is not despair; it is not apathy; it is subtler, quieter than that – it starts as the rubbing of an unworn linen shirt against your skin and slowly becomes the unbearable squeeze of a pair of dress shoes you’ve walked in too long. 

At any point you can choose to get off, though it feels as if you cannot.

It starts with asking yourself the right question. 

I’ve long thought I didn’t have the right answers. 

Then one night, it occurred to me, in a moment as fleeting as any other, ready to be seized or float away – it was never about the answers; it was always about the questions. 

Even the machines we've built from ourselves work this way: ask the wrong question and you spin your wheels for hours; ask the right one and something remarkable happens.

I don’t need all of my soil to be loam when I rise. 

The holes of my past self may never be full again, but inside them lie the questions I ought to ask for new life to grow.

Maybe, the aim is not to seize the day, but instead to be there when the day is seized.
