Culture is elegant. An encapsulation of many factors.

As I sat in restaurants, on park benches, and stumbled through icy forests, there’s been time to think. Alone with yourself you observe those little details which easily get lost amidst the fast-pace of modern life.

How in an instant I can be captured by an emotion and completely lose where or even who I am. How my emotions, and the thoughts plus sensations which trigger them, arise on their own, and entitled as they are, expect to be immediately obeyed.

How truly I have no idea what I am doing at any moment in life, I am just working through more difficult levels in more sophisticated ways than I completed the last one.

The worst part of all this is, when you observe yourself close — the lies, the excuses — they fall away. And you’re left with the raw, complicated truth of a person: a mixture of good, bad, and emotional impulsive monkey.

In one of these restaurants I came across a line of mine from a poem, “Am I forgetting to Hope / Or am I Hoping to forget.” Recounted after my time last year being in a feverish, albeit happy dream.

I would ask: why not choose comfort over truth; why not lie to myself to protect the worldview that brings joy and happiness; why pop such a bubble?

We throw around big words: honour, duty, purpose, meaning. Noble in sentiment. Our attempts to distill the essence of reality.

But often I believe they serve as a way to justify not getting the best outcome.

A set of words which trap us on an elevator not of our creation.

We head up, following the dictums of those who we believe understand life better than we do. We look around, seeing that others there signed up for the same dodgy elevator ride we did, now stuck on what appears to be the thirtieth floor, we decide blending in is easier than jumping off.

For just glancing over the edge, you're considered a traitor, abandoning the cause.

Then you jump... realise there never was a building.

So why pop the bubble?

Because that is what it means to be human.

Even if the perfect life could be calculated—rendered as an instruction manual—it would lack the one thing that makes life worth the breath: the unpredictable.

We kick the stupid rules to the devil and choose to embrace a bit of chaos.

We love our culture but find our own voice.

We jump because we’d rather be a raw, impulsive truth than a make-believe dictum.

We jump to see what the monkey does when it finally realises it's free.
