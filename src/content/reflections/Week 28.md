title: "Thirty-Eight Years"
subtitle: "Week 28"

There is a story about a man who lay beside a pool for thirty-eight years.

The water healed, but only if you reached it first.

And for thirty-eight years, he had no one to carry him.

On the thirty-eighth year, someone asked him: do you want to be well?

Strange question to ask a man who has been waiting almost four decades.

But perhaps it is the only question that matters.

I wake to the same lethargic tune again. I grow cold with the silence of this house. I forget what I did day last.

I drive down the same road every day. Wonder if one day I'll fall off the edge of the world. Might be interesting at least.

Life used to have a destination. The path wasn't pretty. I didn't even choose it. But at least I saw the meadow at the end of the road.

Now I stand between paths. I can choose any of them.

All I see is path.

But today, I heard a baby cub whimpering — wounded at the side of the road.

I get out. Take it home. I know nothing of how to nurse it to health.

This is why I never asked for directions.

I wish they had a manual for this.

But after almost losing a hand once or twice, you learn: you don't rush a wounded thing.

You show up. You wait. You let it decide you're safe.a

Months pass. The cub eats, grows, learns the hand means food.

I wake up. The cub needs feeding before anything else.

I drive down the same road every day.

I wonder if the cub would like the edge of the world. Might be interesting for both of us at least.

One morning, the door is open and it has the legs to leave.

It stays.

Never had to account for a full-grown lion in my fuel budget before.

What we do is dictated by what we see.

What we see is dictated by what we value.

Our problems choose us.

It's less clear if Michelangelo saw the angel in the stone, or if the angel saw him.

Maybe this is why it is said, “an angel spoke to me last night.”

A man told me this: "The cub had been lying there for days. Whimpering so all could hear. You drove past it three times before you stopped."

I said, "What changed?"

He lay his hand gently on my shoulder, patted it twice, turned around, and I never saw him again.

Three days is better than four. Thirty-eight years is better than thirty-nine. And perhaps this is the machinery of grace.

The cub was always crying. The angel was always speaking. The water was always there.

The only thing that changed was the man.
