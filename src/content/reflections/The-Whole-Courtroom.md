I have sinned. Forgive me Father that this has led me back to You.

I am cut and bruised and none around can see. You do. Right?

In this cell I’ve created for myself, I stand before my altar, my judge, my jury, my executioner. I do not kneel.

For I did nothing wrong.

I only defended what I’d built, the walls I’m surrounded by now.

---

I woke up on Monday, one week into my new job, at two in the morning. Then at three. Then at six. I was out of action until my flatmate, who had chosen this as his lie-in day, brought me paracetamol.

I lay around all day wondering, “Why me?”

A dry cough persisted the rest of the week.

I tried to go in, show my face, put my best foot forward this early into the job.

I coughed on everyone.

The work?

Not much better.

My mind was as fractured as my agentic fleet.

I was juggling one hundred tasks at once, attention split, speaking to an intelligence in the cloud.

I was praying that if I asked the wrong questions nicely enough, the problems I couldn’t name would be solved.

I don’t know what I was thinking.

That’s where the self-reproach crept in.

My own mind showed me the life I could lead: repetitive labour, never learning.

The crowd watched and waited for the verdict.

They demanded an answer.

They began to torch the courtroom.

An empire burned, and with it, everything I would not kneel for.

In my mind, I’m the whole courtroom.
