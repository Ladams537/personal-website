---
title: "Walking Into Dark Rooms"
subtitle: "Week 14"
date: 2025-11-30
tags: ["Reflection", "Growth"]
---

Life demands we look forward, but looking back is seductive. The past is safe; it has a fixed quality—an outcome we already know. The future is just a dark room we have to walk into.

If 2025 taught me anything, it is how to walk into increasingly dark rooms without panic.

In university, I treated uncertainty like an error code. I tried to shortcut learning by reverse-engineering the answers, realising too late that I hadn't learned to think—I had only learned to follow instructions.

But real growth is messy. It lives in the chaotic unknown, not in the answer key. It is a place where no one knows the answers. A place where, maybe, there is no 'right' answer, no instructions—your only asset is your mind, your ability to think.

This week was a testament to that chaos. After days of extreme, grinding productivity, my body pulled the emergency brake. I got sick.

But fever has a way of enforcing perspective. Stripped of the world’s productivity guilt, I was forced to simply watch.

Outside, I saw families indifferent to the cold, parents laughing while their children wrestling in the frost. There is a specific, sharp joy in the air leading up to Christmas—a light often missed by adults blinded by their own worries.

It took stopping completely to realise I was misspending my attention.

November was a brutal month of duality. I didn't just ‘live’ this year; I bled. I went all-in more than once, and I lost the hand every time.

I’d like to say I’m a quick learner, but the bruises say otherwise. In losing those hands, I suffered. But through that suffering, I found the courage to stand up again and again.

I learned that you have to be loud enough to rattle snakes. If you aren't attracting enemies or failures, you aren't beating the right bushes. You are walking down a paved road. And if you take no risks, you haven't just stood still—you’ve gone backward.

I am not holding onto 2025, but I am keeping the scars. I learned about power, love, respect, and loss.

When you lose people, you lose the version of yourself they unlocked. I felt that absence.

But I also learned what Thor learned: the hammer was never the source of the power; it only taught him how to focus it. The passion is always there.

George Bernard Shaw once wrote that all progress depends on the "unreasonable man" who refuses to adapt to the world.

So, I choose to be unreasonable. I choose passion over safety. It is not tragic to crash while doing what you love; what is tragic is to rust.
