I didn't enlist. None of us do at that age. Other people pinned the badges on before I could read what they said.

Smart. Funny. Big-hearted — that last one pulling double duty in a way a child can feel but can't name.

I didn't choose a rank. I was assigned one, and I reported for duty every morning without questioning the uniform.

By my teens, I'd gone AWOL — only to build a bunker of my own.

A screen over the entrance like a blast door. Simulations all day: conversations I'd never have, confidence I could rehearse but never deploy.

A pretty girl was incoming fire.

A cool kid was enemy territory.

I mapped the whole world as hostile and called my isolation a strategic position.

University upgraded the illusion.

Bunker to command tower — glass walls, better vantage.

I studied philosophy like battle plans, spoke about suffering though never in the field, discussed death as an event that happened only to others.

Then I shipped out and found out — theory disintegrates on contact. Knocked down: no simulation. Got back up: no training can prepare you for that.

Here's what they never tell you in basic training. The stories you repeat — I'm the smart one, the loner, the prodigy — they are armour.

Real armour. They protected you once.

But it was only when I couldn't carry my armour anymore, when I ran from the simulations and models I had known, that I turned around and saw clearly: armour weighs you down.

You crawl through life instead of running. You're still instead of becoming.

Things happen to you and you call it fate.

You hold your ground in no man's land and call it bravery.

I'm in the barracks now. The coffee in my hands is a blend I didn't know I was brewing: every version of myself stirred into one cup.

The scared kid, the bunker rat, the glass tower tactician, the man who finally touched dirt.

None of them gone. All of them present.

A cadet walks in. I know that face. The rigid posture of someone performing a rank they were assigned, not one they earned.

I slide him a cup.

Stop rehearsing. Deploy.
