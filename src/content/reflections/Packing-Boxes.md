The act of being stretched is where we meet our maker.

No, I don’t mean God.

I mean the idea which sews us up.

It shows itself rarely.

This week I met it: work trials, projects being built, moving out of my house, and flying back to my country of origin.

I was packing boxes, chatting on FaceTime, when awareness came to me, the kind where there is no body. Just for a moment, you glimpse your own non-existence. You see the idea holding up your frame on fine strings. You witness your own separation from a distance where everything looks clear, and your illusion of self-direction vanishes: multiple versions of you, dangling, strung up, puppets moving at different speeds. And you choose to let one move to the idea’s rhythm.

Then I found myself at my going-away get-together. Surprisingly good. You know when you invite friends who haven’t all met each other, you like them all but aren’t sure how they’ll come together, and it goes better than you expected? Yeah. One of those nights.

A few of us had gone through similar trials, at different ages, with different intensities. I sat there thinking (it’s always at joyous times that painful self-reflection and longing find me), “Maybe we don’t stop changing as we age. We just stop risking it, because we remember the suffering of past openness more vividly than its fruit.”

After the storm of the week comes the flood of vulnerability: a quiet time, invisible to others unless I want them to see it, but powerful and present all the same. First I hear the voice of past hurt, whispering, “Here’s how that turned out last time.” Then comes the voice of present hurt: “It’s been a long week. You deserve to get off.” Lastly, the voice of future hurt: “What if you don’t?”

I’m there, in a joyous moment, participating in the idea, where life should feel easy, yet every sensation in my body is trying to deceive me, as if my body itself doesn’t believe we can be this. The more I participate, the further I stray from the path I know. The further I live out this idea, the stronger the most rebellious puppet becomes. And before I can let go, he reaches for my strings, desperate to break free from the beat, so I spend less time somewhere I don’t know and more time in a place that I do. He means well. Perhaps he’s part of the evolution too.

I’ve ended the week trying to dream a little. To relish all that could be. To enjoy the precipice of vulnerability for what it is, and to open myself fully to the idea, allowing it to consume me.

Are dreams just a healthy man’s mirage? Honestly, maybe. Many times they have been. But they are also the very vulnerability that could acclimatise my body to the unknown evolution of the idea. And if some of those dreams die along the way, that’s what they’re for: to let parts of the idea die so the whole of it can live.
