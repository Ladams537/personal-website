subtitle: "Week 16"

I did my first poetry event this week. I shared an unreasonable poem.

Why? I don’t know. Was I simply unprepared? Maybe. Or maybe I needed to be reminded of what has been. It was the poem I wrote to ask out a woman I loved—a relationship that is over now, for the best, but the ghost of it remains.

Reading it paralysed me.

I am usually an incredibly confident public speaker. I have stood in the pulpit of a church, preaching to hundreds without stuttering. I competed at elite levels of games, weightlifting, and even academically.

In all those arenas, I am safe. Those are intellectual challenges. I know the rules. Earning approval has a clear path.

But reading this poem to twenty people? I transformed. I shrank.

I realised that one person does not mean one persona.

The night before the reading, I went to a small church by Kings Cross for a concert. Standing there, listening to sad songs, I didn't feel like the high-achiever. I felt messed up. I felt like my life was a trail of failures.

Not objective failures—but moral and spiritual ones.

For years, I buried my head in the sand. I didn't dig my heels in for the axioms I built my life on; I dug them in to get love from others and avoid my insecurities. I would frame what I want as an intellectual proposition rather than baring my soul and being proud of who I is. I spent years apologising for myself—a pattern I still see in myself from time to time.

I was scared of being rejected by people who had come to love the version of me they thought they knew.

I’ve been trying to be easier to understand, to fit the mould. But I see now that to do so would be to go against the very thing I accuse others of: being too scared to remove the mask and unashamedly live.

The world changes fast, but human desire changes slow: To be seen. To be loved for the person behind the achievements. To be loved for the person you can become.

Poetry is my answer. A fuzzy but bare soul put into a format that cannot be judged by analytics and metrics, only by its truth.

I think this aligns with ambition. Real ambition isn't just climbing a ladder or getting a rank. The highest ambition is to stare uncertainty in the face and not tremble—to bet on the world you see.

Uncertainty is the barrier to growth. It blocks access to the most valuable things in life.

I felt one of my deepest fears bubble up this week: it wasn't about failing. It was about being seen and still being rejected anyway by someone I love.

A person who has lived the good life should not be afraid of death, for they will never die. And a person who fully embraces life should not be afraid of rejection, for they will never be hidden.

Poetry continues to show me in the most unexpected ways what I should live for.

What is it for you?
