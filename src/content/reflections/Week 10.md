"Man is least himself when he talks in his own person," Oscar Wilde wrote. "Give him a mask, and he will tell you the truth."

Halloween, then, is the great unmasking. This year, for the first time, I tried a new mask—a nod to Jake Sully. An avatar. His story resonates. As a human, he was invisible; as a Na’vi, he became a hero, found love, and led a rebellion. Must be the Irish in me.

I’ve always prided myself on reading the room, but the blue paint was a different lens. The stares I received carried a unique posture. I saw that familiar look on a stranger's face—the one I’ve so often misinterpreted—judgement. I was reminded from my own perspective: I can correctly sense what someone is feeling but be completely wrong about why. We see their avatar, not their soul.

This empathy is a double-edged sword. For years, I used it to justify the behaviour of others, building crosses they never asked me to bear. Learning to stop required a total system override. It required change.

And people can change. I am living proof. The boy I was dreamed of the life I have now. He just didn't know that levelling up, as in any game, means better armour but far bigger monsters. I’d rather be broke than breaking, but the goal isn't just to be better. It is to be getting better. As the psychologist Carl Rogers said, “The good life is a process... a direction, not a destination.” We crave the peak, but speedrunning life just means you’re finished too soon.

This insight highlights my greatest error: I have tried to speed-run others. I wanted to drag them to my destination, forgetting the race was their privilege to run. I fired the starting gun while their shoes were still untied.

There are people running at your speed already. Don’t try and force someone swimming in another lane to match your pace. Don’t try and fit someone into a mould they were never made for. We are all called to great things, but not in everything. Synergistic people exist. As Mother Teresa put it, “I can do things you cannot, you can do things I cannot; together we can do great things.”

Jake Sully was a nobody among humans. Among the Na’vi, he found his people. But he had to earn it. He had to fall, learn, and fight his way to becoming. The lesson isn't to be someone else. It's to find the tribe, the place, and the purpose that make your mask and your face one and the same. Find your people. The fight for what you believe in will follow.
