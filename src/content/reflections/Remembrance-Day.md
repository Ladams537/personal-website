The stories I tell myself are the ones I remember, not the stories that actually happened.

I gallop along on my adventure, riding on horseback.

My armour clinks, the weight carried by my horse I take no notice of, as my mind sits far from the field I am in.

When I wake up from the dream I am in, I realise I no longer recognise this place.

I continued to get more responsibility at work this week.

That really hit me when I got a fresh instance of Claude to review the codebase for new changes. It had no memory of the weeks behind it, and it said, I'm paraphrasing, "You own the whole data pipeline and knowledge layer side of the platform."

I thought to myself, "Oh my, I'm only two weeks in!"

The very life I had worked hard to get was the very life I was living. Yet it's never enough.

I still get hungry, I still get sad, I still think about times in my past, and I wish I had it all.

I saw "Wicker" towards the end of the week.

A husband fashioned out of wicker for a woman who'd never been married, in a conservative village where everyone overlooked her. Everyone but him.

Part of me related to him.

To the way he learned to fit in, to love.

To the way he saw the world.

Open, honest, innocent. With no past to teach him otherwise, he suspected no ill-intent from the woman he'd come to love.

But then there's the other part of me with scars.

That doesn't see the world like he did anymore.

The part of me which braces for impact when life is going well, because I don't trust that it will continue to be so.

The part which has learned not to trust my desires, for they've led me astray.

The part which remembers the innocent side. Sometimes, while both are drifting through the same space and time in their dreams, they collide, if only for a brief moment, and they wonder, "How'd we end up so far apart?"

---

The week we just passed had us marking Remembrance Day on the Sunday before the 11th.

The lives of so many people were changed forever. The effects still ripple through our society today.

And I believe each of us has events in our own timeline that ripple like this. Not the same, nowhere near. But suffering is measured by the one who carries it.

The events we think about when we are riding on the back of a horse, wearing heavy armour.

The kind which captures us in an instant and transports us far from the place we inhabit with our bodies.

Beautiful irony: the memories I escape to are the very things which prevent me from creating more memories in the present.

When I allow myself to go back there, I find that it tends to come back with me. My memories find me in the present. They seek me out like scouts, unaware of who or what they have discovered, only knowing that it is worthy of further investigation.

I play with it, the way a cat plays with a toy mouse, so maybe I deserve it.

So am I surprised on Remembrance Day it chose to come back?

The side of me like the wicker husband goes, "What's the worst that could happen?"

I reopen old pathways, I walk on a road, but I do not know which way I am going. Do I walk to or from the place I seek?

Then, I see it. Is it in my mind? Is it reality finally catching up to my dreams?

The thing I have sought for, the jewel of my eye.

Then, a moment later, the jewel is gone.

My heart is racing, my horse is not slowing down.

The next thing I know, I am getting off my horse, waiting at my next stop, and a wonderful new week awaits me on the other side of this next journey.

I remember what happened on the ride, but I'm not sure if that's just what I want to remember, or if that's what actually happened.
