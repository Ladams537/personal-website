---
title: "The Predictable Unbearable"
subtitle: "Week 24"
date: 2026-02-08
tags: ["Agency", "Change"]
---

Beyond a certain point, even the unbearable becomes bearable—provided it is predictable.

It becomes a burning building you no longer acknowledge, a disaster you’ve mistaken for a home.

We often think agency is the pursuit of goals, but true agency is the wisdom to refuse.

This becomes difficult when an obstacle disguises itself as the prize—appearing as the very thing you would sacrifice everything to obtain.

As you grow smarter, your obstacles evolve with you. They become more sophisticated: a lucrative opportunity, a seductive path, or a dream that the version of you from six months ago would have died for.

As an ambitious person, you will inhabit more burning buildings than most.

Many leave scars—lessons bought with pain. But as you continue to roll the dice, you risk being lured into a deadlier game: one that doesn't just scar you, but threatens to swallow you whole.

I am not immune to this. Nature balances passion with a steep price: if you put your whole heart on the table to play, you risk losing it all.

Sometimes, however, we are granted a moment of grace. Whether you call it luck or divine intervention, the result is the same:

God knew how much you were willing to risk, so He had it removed.

We are pulled out of the wreckage only to realise how destructive the environment truly was.

Yet, standing in the ashes among people who cannot comprehend the heat, the allure to return is irresistible.

We crave the familiar. We edit our memories, deleting the sleepless nights, the disrespect, and the flames, while over-saturating the highlight reels.

The more agentic you become, the more uncertainty you must carry.

I find myself in a constant battle with a brain that prefers a miserable, known past over the terrifying adventure of an unknown future.

Why?

Because that adventure is inherently lonely.

I remember being ranked in the Top 10 in Overwatch. Queue times stretched past sixty minutes. You played the same handful of people every game.

Improvement was no longer measured in hours, but in months.

This is where nostalgia creeps in, whispering, "Wasn't it better back then?"

We expect that reaching the summit will lead us to a utopia of like-minded peers, but growth is a process of differentiation.

You may respect your peers, but you are no longer the same as them.

Those who perish in the fire fail for the same reasons; those who escape are successful for different ones.

It is a lonely road, but I hope you live a life you are proud of. And if you find that you are not, I hope you find the strength to start all over again.
