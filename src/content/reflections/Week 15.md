I overheard a conversation on the Tube this week that stopped me cold. Two women, discussing the arc of their lives.

The first spoke of cycles. She realised she was repeating her mother’s words to her own daughter. She remembered wearing miniskirts in her twenties, thinking she was the main character, only to see years later that none of us are.

The second moment was even better. When the topic turned to retirement, one woman declared: “I will have karaoke at mine. Few will turn up because of it, but at least I know everyone there, including myself, will be having an awesome time.”

What if? Will you regret it? How can you know?

"I don’t know."

It’s a phrase I muttered constantly this week—at a wedding, staring at my code, looking at the ceiling. We cannot know. It is a terrifying rule of existence: the bigger the decision, the more axiomatic the value, the less information you have.

Do you want kids? Will you regret settling down? Who do you want to be?

Existential dread creeps in. But I need not wait to "live." I am already doing it. If I don’t enjoy the coffee that braces my lips now, how can I expect to be a person who appreciates his children later?

This moment is as much life as any other.

So, while I sat in my chair and worked, I made myself uncomfortable. I felt inadequate, lonely, and romantic all at once. And I realised I wouldn’t want it any other way.

There are times my mind plays tricks, telling me these feelings are weakness. But it is amazing how energised I feel simply by looking up and putting one foot in front of the other.

If I have learned anything in 2025, it is that I have already arrived at the point of arriving.

The only thing I need is people in the fight with me. I pass so many souls daily and expect nothing from them. But I am grateful for the few who, despite all their chances to leave, chose to stay.

That loyalty is the only currency that matters. Treasure it. Because if you choose to be anyone other than yourself, you will eventually resent the people who love you for the mask you wear.

Like the woman on the Tube, I have decided. I would rather have the karaoke and an empty room than a full house of strangers.
