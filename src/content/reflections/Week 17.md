History is full of moments that appear small in the wake but prove instrumental to the route you sail.

I circumnavigated my world this year only to find myself back at the starting line. But while the coordinates were the same, the person standing there was not. Time had weathered us both.

I remembered everything. I laughed. I grieved. I took it all in.

On December 22nd last year, I was baptised in the Thames. I remember treading through Borough Market afterward, soaked through, surrounded by people I would come to know all too well. The night ended in a Nando’s, listening to different perspectives of my journey.

While John 4:23 touched my heart most that night, the truth is, my heart had already been stolen before the water dried.

The water of the Thames was the starting point, and that current would carry me to the end of the chapter for a poetic conclusion in Paris on the 19th of April.

It is the "1904" that now marks my handle. Even as my rubber dinghy veered into a sweeping storm—losing my phone by the Seine and navigating the night—I realised that being 'off course' was exactly where I needed to be.

It was one of the most memorable days of my life, in a year that has had its fair share of them. A great memory, I’ve realised, is both a blessing and a curse.

I’ll be honest: I failed more than I succeeded. I betrayed my own vows. But I refused to give up on the man staring back with bruises and salt-streaked cheeks.

This was the first year I forced myself to sit in that "pain of sucking." It is easy to stay safe—to repeat the loops you know—but that is how life passes you by. When you finally look up, it’s gone.

I was tired of the safe, repetitive loops of my past, so when life began to sprint, I didn't just watch—I lunged for it.

I grabbed a life moving faster than I could run. I tripped. I smashed my face and broke my heart.

I learned exactly how much weight I could carry without letting go. Then, the world grew quiet. I turned to see how far I’d come and realised the bruises were the point.

The meaning my heart yearned for—the meaning the earth so often withholds—wasn't something I had to find; it was something I had lived.

I cried, but the salt wasn't bitter. I was content. Life had stopped running away from me, or perhaps I’d finally learned how to keep pace.

For a moment, life greeted me as an equal. Then it took off again—but this time, I was already smiling.

Perhaps this all culminated at my company’s Christmas party this week. The man who stood there was battered by a year of storms, but when I looked in the mirror I saw that the waves hadn't broken me—they had only stripped away the hull to reveal the wood beneath.

At the start of the night, someone remarked, "This guy screams personality." It made me chuckle.

I started the year trying to force myself into structures I thought I needed, only to realise I don't fit in conventional boxes.

I am a rather free spirit. Whether or not that is the spirit God searches for in John 4:23, I cannot say–but it is who I am, and I would have it no other way.
