Unexpected situations have a way of teaching life's most profound lessons, jolting us out of the mundane and into the memorable.

Graduation week was full of surprises—highs, lows, and most importantly, reflection. The transformation I underwent during those four years can only be described as extraordinary. This period of reflection illuminated not only my own journey but also the nature of the relationships that defined it. It brought to mind the quiet contracts we make with one another, the most fundamental of which is the commitment to understand.

Understanding is a pact, a choice to listen before asserting what we believe to be true. This subtle decision underpins much of harmonious cooperation and is the very root of humility.

In reflecting, I realised something beautiful about my relationships with those closest to me: there are no scores kept, no weighty expectations, only a mutual hunger to help each other succeed. Success itself is an elusive quality, difficult to capture in words. In its place, the person behind the achievements—their character and integrity—becomes paramount. We share an unspoken but ever-present mission, relishing in each other's best and remaining steadfast at each other's worst.

Working to better yourself is an act of defiance against the world's inertia; a rebellion against the chains that would tether you to mediocrity. We are all called to great things, a call that few heed with consistency. The strength of the warrior lies not in a single moment of magic, but in embracing the lonely road of repeated failure, of being mocked or ridiculed, and still showing up—because they know it must be done. This strength, however, is rarely found in isolation. It is fortified by a pack of rare, like-minded individuals who, upon finding each other, hold fast, knowing the value of what they have discovered.

As Friedrich Nietzsche wrote in Twilight of the Idols, “He who has a why to live for can bear almost any how.” He also championed the idea that greatness is forged by maintaining a single, focused direction over a long period of time.

The fuel to keep going is not gifts, attention, or even love. The true fuel is respect—a quiet acknowledgement of who you are, from those few whose opinions truly matter.

It’s often said that self-respect is the necessary precondition for self-improvement. As a young person—and I am still guilty of this at times—I would get lost in the persona that others saw. I would fall for my own mask, believing that this facade, what they perceived as my best self, was who I was truly called to be. What I failed to see was that this was only a part of me. Held in its proper place, a persona can be a gift to the world, but I would lose sight of my whole self. I would lose respect for who I was, until a moment of despair when I could no longer look myself in the eye and say I was following my path.

This realisation rarely dawned on me by my own accord; instead, it was often a simple moment, a comment from an unsuspecting person, that changed everything. These people, often in my life for an important yet temporary time, served a purpose much like Thor's hammer, Mjölnir. As Odin taught his son, the hammer was never the source of his power, but a tool to help him channel it. Similarly, these individuals helped me learn to control and direct my own growing strength across time, each teaching me different, yet equally important lessons.

The most beautiful moment of my graduation was reading the yearbook. A brief comment from a young man I had reached out to during his time of need. He never took me up on the offer for coffee, yet under the heading ‘What friends say,’ I saw his words: “Am sincerely grateful for your emotional support during my darkest moments.”

Save yourself, see yourself, and you will never know who else you might lift up with you.
