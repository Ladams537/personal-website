Most of the time, I have no idea what I am doing. I am an amalgamation of passions whirling inside, pulling me in conflicting directions.

It took less than a week in a new country for my mind to adapt to driving on the right. The past was simply overwritten. Watching 21 Jump Street with friends this week hammered this home. What was "cool" when the characters went to school was repulsive less than a decade later. Times change, and definitions shift.

If I can forget the rules of the road without a conscious reminder, how much easier is it to forget who I am when entering an environment that values different things? We try to cling to our perspective of events that will never be again. We write down our principles, yet the slightest trigger throws us back into spirals.

“No man ever steps in the same river twice, for it’s not the same river and he’s not the same man.” – Heraclitus

That sadness manifested as the age-old question repeatedly slapped me across the face: “What is love?”

I found myself returning to Luke 15—the Parable of the Prodigal Son. I love her. But love is not a transient emotion. I realise that perhaps the most loving thing is not to chase, but to let go. To let God love; not to play God. Just as my soul veered over the edge, weighed down by anger, a strange voice pulled me back, saying, “It is okay.”

This is the magic: simply not giving up on yourself.

Yet, the silence creates space for a darker voice. “No. You cannot do that.” A sentence I have heard many times in my life. From outside and then later from within. A sentence which awakens a special kind of madness. A madness that drives obsession.

“He who fights with monsters should look to it that he himself does not become a monster, and if you gaze long enough into an abyss, the abyss will gaze back into you.” – Friedrich Nietzsche

Obsession for the wrong reasons is a rusted sword. It does the job but kills you in the process. Each swing takes a piece of your soul. I read a quote from Marc Maron today which beautifully encapsulates this: “The monster I created to protect the kid inside me is hard to manage.”

Thankfully, it is rarely too late to change the weapon in your hand. The monster feeds on the masks we wear to protect ourselves. The only viable path is to put the sword down and just be. As the saying goes, “The hardest mask to wear is your own.”
