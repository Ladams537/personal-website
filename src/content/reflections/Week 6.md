I am exactly where I need to be.

This past week, I found myself in a curious, paradoxical state: I have nothing to live for, and yet I feel a genuine, electric excitement to simply live. I allowed myself the luxury of purposelessness, and in that space, I unearthed the joy, the meaning, and, most importantly, the silence I was seeking. It was a fundamental shift from a state of becoming to a state of being—from relentless questioning to quiet, solid knowing. It became the unshakable foundation on which to build my kingdom.

In the chaos of stopping my endless search, I found the direction I was looking for. A break from chasing the answers made the obvious ones become, well—obvious.

We make life unnecessarily complicated.

Greatness often lurks behind the shadow of obsession.

The key insight is that one doesn't need to be obsessed indefinitely or across every domain simultaneously. Consider the multitude of people who have excelled at a plethora of things throughout their lives, accomplishing feats many of us only dream of. At any given point, however, their focus was laser narrow. This narrow focus enables rapid, exponential improvement. Once a level of mastery is achieved, the focus can responsibly widen to integrate that new skill into the wider pool of expertise they've developed over their lifetime.

I’m guilty of making two common mistakes in this pursuit:
1. Mindless Obsession: This is obsession that disregards the details—the cousin of madness. It leads to making one thing the epicentre of life, repeating the same mistakes on a loop without ever stepping out of the passenger seat to reflect. Norman Vincent Peale said, "The trouble with most of us is that we would rather be ruined by praise than saved by criticism."
2. Childish Clawing: The frantic need to try and master every skill at once. I aspire to be proficient in so many roles: writer, dancer, parent, partner, friend, coder, leader, speaker, athlete, and more. I know I can excel at each of them, and for some, I might already be at an "elite" status. Yet, the mountain goes infinitely higher, and trying to climb all of them at once means getting permanently stuck at the base of each.

Obsess.

Don't let the word scare you; intense focus is not inherently bad. The world need not understand your driven concentration. Be an artist of your soul. "The indispensable first step to getting the things you want out of life is this: decide what you want." - Ben Stein.

And here’s the best part: what you want will change as you move through life. Embrace that. Don’t cower from it. If you're not slightly uncomfortable with who you once were, you're probably not growing fast enough.

As was said in The Imitation Game, "Sometimes it's the people no one imagines anything of who do the things that no one can imagine." To one day find your true home in the world, you must be willing to be rejected by it entirely for a time. This is a journey we all must undertake as adults, or risk living a life that is not truly our own.

The good news is that no matter where you are—beginning, middle, or end—you can choose at any moment to follow that voice that has been within you the whole time. For some of us, that voice roars loud in the night, keeping us awake until we follow it. For others, it has been subdued to a whisper. No matter how distant it might seem.

Find it.

I wish I could say I'll see you there, but our mountains are not the same. I do look forward to hearing about the climb, for that is how you’ll’ve earned your new name.
