This life I take for granted.

Years I have been blessed by friends, health, memory, life. When is it enough?

I got to see James Arthur this week. Threw me right back to childhood. A little boy watching him sing on the XFactor.

I reverse-engineered my path from here to there, walking backwards through the steps I'd taken forward.

I was thirteen again listening to Just Say You Won't Let Go with my holiday long-distance “relationship”.

I had to flatten what I felt into the words of another because I didn't yet possess the keys to access mine.

I looked down more streets.

Some I was ashamed to look at. Others I wish I could go back to more often.

I saw faces I'd forgotten. Chatted with those I did miss.

I'd look at my map and see the contour lines.

The no-go areas: the electric rooms where sparks ignited cities; the empty rooms where loneliness was never-ending; the excavated rooms.

I hold these close in thought though they can be far away in time.

Vital waypoints that light the desire line ahead.

After exploring what I already knew, it was time to venture out to the frontier. But my map doesn't contain a North star. Just a lot of South ones.

Paths trodden by those before me, signposts now out-of-date.

I spent so much time looking at the map that I forgot I could just look at the terrain myself.

So I looked up.

The land had more biodiversity than the legend allowed for.

Whole species of feeling I'd filed under the wrong name or not named at all.

People I'd have categorised as a known species because that's what my mental model demanded.

Six months ago, I'd have glanced at my map and changed route without ever looking up — same territory, different location.

Now, I could discover new land. Scary, beautiful, new. Land.

To be a joker around one friend, and serious around another.

An engineer in one place, a poet in another.

To know what you are doing in the morning and have absolutely no idea by the time the sun sets.

The boy who dreamed of all of this: he got it. Every last thing.

And now he stands on the summit he pointed at from the valley floor, and the only thing he can see clearly is how much further the land extends in every direction.

I don't know if I'm at the right place at the right time.

I don't know if the map is even current.

The legend has changed and I can't tell if that's growth or just drift.
