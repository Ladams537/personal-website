Walking through Margravine,
Not expecting to find anything 
Significant. 
Just a bad house viewing—
My former lover haunting the corner— 
I was hopeless right as I saw her.
Not a ghost, not a memory, 
Just a girl turning the silent stones into a stage. 
Playing in a graveyard. 
Giddy, glistening: delighting. 
And amid the soundlessness of that setting,
An image was scorched into my heart: 
The world was her playground. 
Mine was just... waiting... to be found.

I used to believe Santa Clause came down my chimney
And the tooth fairy put money under my pillow with me.
I believed I could be:
Cowboy, Soldier, Sir Lancelot, King. 
That being best-in-class meant 
I’d conquer the world with a plastic sword. 
I believed I was invisible. 
That demons only came out when I was bored. 
And I did all of this... **without paying rent.**

Then the wings were clipped. 
The kitchen table became a border crossing
Where I bartered grades for gaming. 
My parents: gardeners with shears. 
The image of who I should be—
In their heads—was so clear. 
I sat in strict pews, a church of 'No,'
Until my spirit was just a muffled echo. 
I broke a heart, I broke a faith, 
And I listened to people who had given up on themselves
Tell me to do the same. 
I retired behind a screen, hiding in the UI,
A passenger in the vehicle of my own life.

As I grew, the rent came due. 
I thought childhood was a vault that would never empty—
That I was immortal, an heir to an endless century.
But the clock is a debt collector, and her hands move fast; 
It turns the "forever" of youth into a "how long will it last?"

Covid struck—a silent, static tide. 
Suddenly, I wasn't walking through Margravine; 
I was living it in everything. 
Old opportunities I had squandered were buried in that land, 
And I saw the gravestones of the many lives I’d let go down.
The engineer, the artist, the boy with the sword— 
All buried in the silence I was afraid of being bored. 

London: a different world, a new land,
A chance to excavate my playground.
Landed myself on personal training,
All the "cool kids" were day trading.
I got to workout and post about it, 
Share reels, invest in myself, market.
I attracted an audience I didn't expect: 
Men following, making 'special requests'.
Engineer, not a model. So I shifted gears,
Hid away again, confronted childhood fears.
I was a ghost in a city that didn't know my name, 
Trying to pay a debt for a life I forgot how to claim.

The demons that came out when I was bored?
Had found a promotion; they had a new Lord.
They didn’t hide under the bed or behind my drawers; 
They wore sharp suits and waited at the chapel doors. 
I swapped my boredom for a mandate, my silence for a shout, 
I joined a cult and found out... what "belonging" was about.

Ten rounds with Satan: spiritually beaten, emotionally bruised
Wondering, "How did I end up at the front of this room?"
I was visibly invisible—a cadet for a cause,
Tribulation the reward I toiled for transmuting the world's laws.
They tried to name my wife, to draft my life on a map: 
So I left the keys to that kingdom under the iron ribs of the Eiffel Tower.

And then... there was her. Not a mandate, not a cause, 
The exception to whom I told my heart.
A kindred spirit who walked into my dark with a lantern I didn’t recognise. 
She was a saint among the sharp-suited demons— 
A soft, sacred breath in a room full of schmucks. 
I told her I was a dreamer, a romantic born in the wrong century. 
I carved her name into a new kind of gravestone: 
Carrying along a debt I do not regret.
But I do wonder... Am I forgetting to hope... or hoping to forget?

Hope did come back, but the debt was still there, 
Hanging like smoke in the London air, 
Inflation was a monster, making a mockery of my bread. 
The interest on my soul was mounting; my spirit was in the red. 
So I thought: 
"To hell with it. If I’m a ghost, I’ll be a poltergeist. 
If the world is a thief, I’ll perform a glorious heist."

I walked to the glass, asking for an amortised allowance, 
Offering nothing but deadly defiance. 
I stood before the teller—no mask, no gun, 
Just the crushing debt of everything I hadn't become. 

I brought the gravestones of the many lives I’d let go down. 
I brought the 'special requests' and the weight of the London town. 
I brought the painful nights that I'd hoped to forget,
I brought the weight of a world that hadn't snapped just yet.

I pushed the ledger through the slot, 
The record of every 'No' I’d swallowed and every battle I’d fought. 
I waited for the siren. I waited for the shame. 
I waited for her to ask why I couldn't just play their game.

But the teller didn't reach for the alarm. She didn't even flinch. 
She just took my ledger, inch by jagged, blood-stained inch. 
She didn’t look for the engineer, the cadet, or the dreamer— 
She looked at the man who was finally a believer 
Not in a cult or a cause, but in the cost of the soul. 
She picked up my debt... and she ripped it in half.

I walked out of that bank with my head held high. 
No longer a debtor, no longer just a ghost flickering in the UI. 
I went back to Margravine, where the stones are heavy and grey, 
And I saw a man walking 'in his head'—the exact same way I used to. 
He looked like a cadet for a cause that had already failed. 
Exhausted. Carrying a world that was warped, wrangled and worn.

I wanted to stop him. I wanted to tell him: we don’t know the length of our lease. 
So stop looking for the exit. Find true north. 
Claim a version of peace that doesn't require the world's signature—
Because the truth is, none of us out here know what we’re doing.
If your dreams are ghosts, give them blood. Give them bone. 
Don't have them standing over you when your payments come due,
Whispering, "We were always here. We were waiting for you."

And the broken dreams? The nights of wrangled despair?
Those aren't arrears. Those are the receipts. They prove you lived. 

I didn't hand him a sermon, or a mandate, or a sword.
Just a simple, kind word and the light I’d clawed back from the dark.
I watched the cold carcass of every mask I’d once worn sink into that soil—
I let them be ghosts... So I didn't have to be one anymore.

The debt in the end wasn't a burden to be outrun; 
It was the price of admission to becoming someone. 
I became the playground. I became the spark 
For the next soul wandering in that soundless dark.

The Bank is finally quiet. The ledger is clear. 
I even take the teller out for dinner once a year— 
Because where I saw a failure... she saw a dreamer. 
And where I saw an end? She saw a beginner.
