title: "How It Feels"
date: 2026-02-27

He wanted what a boy wants. 
And he was a boy.

He wanted the woman who
turned heads like car crashes.
He wanted to be consumed
by she-hasn't-texted-back
and does-she-even-like-me?
He wanted instagram filters
permanently applied. Hips
so wide
ships could pass between.

I don't blame him. 
He didn't know what love
looked like. Never seen
two people see each 
other through their eyes, 
without first connecting
with their phones.

Snapchat taught him geometry,
gaming taught him teamwork,
Covid told him his worth.
He learned to hide behind
fancy words and lustred appearances,
while rusting on the inside.

I knew this boy. 
I fed him, watered him,
Let him run the ship.
And he drove it into the most beautiful
rocks you've ever seen.

He had a screen that showed 
more beauty before breakfast 
than his grandfather witnessed 
in a full life.

So he calibrated. 
He set 
his threshold 
at the place where your blood hums 
and your sleep disappears 
and you'd give anything 
for one more hour 
in the blast radius of her attention.

He called this love.

And so when I found
myself stranded beside
a woman not like he knew.
A woman who demanded
I not shy from my inside.
I could not hide.
She didn't tell me I was impressive.
Didn't respond to my flirting 
with a calculated touch.
Didn't apologise for her opinions.

She just watched armour fall
to rusted edges underneath—
digging into my skin 
since I learned 
my worth.

She walked beside me.
The walking was the thing.
Performance waxed.
Performance waned.
My heart rate for the first time:
stayed the same.
Seeing this, it raced.
Perplexed. Why? 
Let me be here
I complained!

Stopped running.
Stood in-between
insufficiency and becoming,
without running to any destination
because I did not know this place.
In this silence, I arrived.

How do you be
when your being falls short
of the lustred armour you wore 
as a boy?

How do you sit
in the almost, the not yet,
the space between interest, and certainty—
without flinching? 

How do you learn to be 
someone's Tuesday 
when you've spent your whole life 
chasing someone's Saturday night?

How do you 
outgrow a boy's fast food romance 
for a man's 
Tuesday night dinner table?

I didn't know, I don't know,
I can't put it in a poem
or a song, or a neat bundle
for you to take and carry on.

But I do
know how it feels.
