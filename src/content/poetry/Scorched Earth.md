She doesn't snore, doesn't *stir*,
even asleep she's *considerate*—
turns away, gives me *air*,
asks nothing, *needs* nothing,
loves me the way a good wife *should*.

And I've hated myself for *years*
for wishing she'd ask for *more*—
scream, break something, *need* me
in a way that isn't so... *reasonable*.

Toes hit the *carpet*—beach, Normandy—
wrangled out of *barbed wire*,
bedroom to hallway to *freedom*—

Equip my Harry Potter *nightgown*,
pull up my *silk socks*,
slide on my *sliders*—
frazzled, fated *adrenaline*
yanks my shoulders *back*:
player one *ready*.

Reach the *hilltop*.
Hand hovers over the *mouse*.
Character standing *idle*
in a digital city I *chose*
because it looked like *hers*—
the one we said we'd move to to*gether*.

I don't even *play* now—
just *stand* there,
watching other players.

And something in my *chest*—
not my heart, something *else*—
*tightens*.
Then *releases*.
Like a fist I've been *clenching* for years
suddenly remembering it could *open*.

I look out at a world un*raveling*.
This fortress I built
one Amazon box at a *time*—
cor*roded*
by the sea of *seeing*.

I stand—
chair rolls, hits the wall—
hands vibrating—
like they've been waiting,
screaming for permission to move.

Want to smash the monitors—
all three—
but instead I'm laughing,
grotesque, grimace, sublime,
animal, unhinged, mine.

Headphones clatter—
pacing—
blood pulsating out of my body armour—
laughter twists—
trapped in my throat since
the mortgage,
the promotion,
"we should try for a baby,"
the last time I felt like myself.

Grab the energy drink—
$1.99 for liquid *alertness*—
*crush* it—
metal *screams*—
sticky on my palm, 
feeling my own *hands*,
my own *strength*,
pathetic as it *is*—
this tiny act of subjugation
*mine*—

The fan continues its *spray*— 
machine gun *strafing*— 
and my outfit— 
this *ridiculous* nightgown— 
now feels like a *cape*,
and I realise—
*all along*—
I've been a *latent* player
in my own *game*.

Forehead against 
the cold *glass*.
Street *empty*.
3:47 *AM*.
*Silence*.

Ten years.
All I did was conquer—
three-bedrooms instead of two,
the driveway, the fence, the *yard*

And now I stand victorious
over a kingdom ravaged
by my own war.

All of it scorched.
All of it mine.

The world doesn't *know* I'm unraveling.
The world doesn't *need* me to hold it together.

I *waged* a war of my own *creation*,
spirited, *splendid*, spectacular—
only casualties were me and *her*.

Kitchen table. *Pen*. *Paper*.
Grocery lists, permission slips,
evidence of *functioning*.

I write: "I'm—"

I'm sorry.
I'm leaving.
I'm not who you thought.
I'm exactly who you thought,
But would not admit.

Cross it out.

How do you explain
that you've been drowning in slow motion,
that the life you built
was built by someone who *died*—
years ago—
and you've just been *piloting his corpse*?

Crumple the paper.

No words for this.
The *silence* will have to speak for me.

There is one thing I need to *take* 
Though. One thing that was never *theirs*.

Bedroom *dark*.
She doesn't *stir*.
Baby's *breath*—
that small *rhythm*—
continues without *me*.

Move to the *closet*,
past her sleeping *form*,
to the shoebox labeled
"Tax Documents *2019*"
that has never contained tax *documents*.

Inside: her *letter*.
Her *handwriting*.
The night I chose *safety* over *her*.

Hold it to my *chest*—
three seconds,
*four*,
*five*—
only honest thing in this *house*.

Pocket it.
*Silent*.

Maybe I'll knock on her *door*.
Maybe I'll just sit by the *river*.
Maybe I'll realise—
I'm not running *toward* her—
I'm running *from*
the man who gave her *up*.

Baby *cries*.
I hear it through the wood.

I do not turn *around*.

She will *wake*.
Find the empty *bed*.
Call my phone
(*already on airplane mode*).

Confused before *angry*,
worried before *hurt*—
because she *loves* me.

And I have never loved her
the way she *deserves*.

She will try to *fix* this.
She will *want* to understand.
She will offer *therapy*, *compromise*, *time*.

But there is nothing left to *save*.

Leaving is the first *honest* thing
I've done in *years*.
