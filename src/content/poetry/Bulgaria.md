Lacking excitement a plane I boarded,
Flying to a destination I did not care for.
The airport toilets filthy,
And one unspecial taxi ride,
To a room where I could hear the cars outside all night.

Constructing my way to work,
Three tired sheep huddled close.
They looked at me, lacking glee,
Hoping they could just go to the hotel again and die.

Training: for depression? Drawn-out
Sessions, improving creativity
For Rabia and I had to create our own lessons.
The bell would ring—freedom! Or
Just another trip to paradise mall.
I’d eat and silently retreat into time’s sweet, soft, serene beat.

Central Sofia. City unlocked.
Smoking, secrets, and sweet lamb.
We sat all night trying to understand
Each other. A task at hand,—
Balkan Hector set loose,
A mirror of Miles murmuring all night.
English pretty-boy with a sweetheart.

But militancy would come at a cost:
A weight weighing watchfully on my soul.
Old habits, die hard,
As one tries hard to run from what they are.
The more you repress,
The more the lion grows
Until you sow a monster mightier than you are.
Shame. A drug which goes down easy,
Because the next night you repeat it,
And fall into night,
The light retreats,
And despite the momentary exhalation
The same cars still drive down those streets.

Sulking will not do. Get back up,
Your platoon needs you.
Those sheep need encouragement,
Yet they prove a tough bunch to excite!

Getting to know the Bulgarian team,
An interesting team it would be.
Complicated love stories with their work,
Loud, boisterous—depressed.
Freedom awaits those brave enough to leap.
The stars were in sight if only they looked up
And saw their beauty reflect on their feet.

A passenger princess I became.
Beside a woman who I’d realise was the same,
Soul as me. One who cared deeply,
And so the sins of those against her linger further
**Within. A heart which feels everythin’,**
**Must be very careful who they give it to.**
Cold exteriors only work when they are true,
For cracks in the armour do always shine through,
For those who have been brave enough to look at their own scars.
She jokes and jibes, hiding behind those sly remarks.
But they who save others from sin
Must be careful they don’t end up in
The same place.

Week two flew through,
Simple, sweet, and clean.
I’d walk in, do my work,
Relax, and go home.
Peaceful it was,
Peace I was shown.
Too focused to remember the sin,
Too happy for this life I lived.

Wednesday would come round,
A whirlwind of memories,
Sound, and simmering sadness.
Swords clanked, deafening to only one ear,
As I would veer over the city of Sofia.
Puzzle pieces scattered on the board,
History, maths—I don’t want to hear that no more.
The ladies ran off, I was lost,
The last proper night and this is it?

Little did I know that the show was about to begin.
Iveta was lookin’ for a simple Scottish bloke,
The train was out of sight,
Yet, spirited she would tell of Bulgaria’s spell,
On men.
Crying with laughter, I felt bad,
But how could I be sad,
When her stories were so fucking mad.
A different world from what I knew,
Though the stars had before shown me that view.

I’d head inside, think of my many lives
Lived this year. Clear divides:
Blurred lines; beautiful irony; broken identity.
Who am I?  
A conflicted entity of passions and pain,
Wanting to spare anyone from seeing that again.
I guess I hide it well (most of the time),
Trying instead to turn caterpillars into butterflies.

Balkan Hector did not disappoint,
The club, even if terrible,
Would get him having a good time.
Moments I won’t forget,
From this trip,
As I shut my brain off,
Danced the night all
The way
Till the end.

The final full day awaits.  
A buzzing butterfly I jump out
To head to one last set of lectures.
A long day, an anti-climactic ending,
As my sheep all went home again.
Here I am, is this it?  
But two allies would pull me from the wreckage
Dust me off and say, “shall we?”
Old moustaches, happiness, and Rakia
Flowed out of the tap bottle into my throat.
Letting my hair down, doing and saying things
I’m not sure I should say in front of colleagues,
But hey ho, life is to be lived, not just written.
